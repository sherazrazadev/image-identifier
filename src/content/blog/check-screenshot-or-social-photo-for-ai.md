Most people do not have the original file. They have a **screenshot**, a WhatsApp forward, or a crop from Instagram or TikTok. Then they search how to check if that picture is **AI-generated**.

You can still run a [free AI vs real check](/). Expect more **Unclear** results, and treat the answer as a weaker hint than when you have the original photo.

## Why social copies are harder

Every share can change the file:

- Screenshots add UI chrome, status bars, and extra compression
- Apps crop, filter, or re-save the image
- Forwards stack those losses again

The picture you see may still *look* like the original. The file is not the same. Any checker — including ours — has less to work with.

## What to check when you only have a repost

1. Ask for the original download (camera roll or “Save image”), not a new screenshot of your screen.
2. If that is impossible, use the **largest, cleanest** copy you can get — crop out chat bubbles and app chrome when you can do so honestly.
3. Drop a JPEG or PNG (max 15 MB) into [Image Identifier](/).
4. Read **Likely AI-generated**, **Likely real**, or **Unclear** as an estimate, not proof.

No account. The file you select is not uploaded to our servers for analysis. See [Privacy](/privacy/) and [Free AI image checker — no upload](/blog/free-ai-image-checker-no-upload/).

## What the result means on a screenshot

| Result | On a social copy |
| --- | --- |
| Likely AI-generated | Extra caution. Still do not accuse someone on this label alone. |
| Likely real | Looks more like a real photo — a viral caption can still be false. |
| Unclear | Common for screenshots. Try a better file or another source. |

Checkers can be wrong both ways. Details: [How accurate are AI image detectors?](/blog/how-accurate-are-ai-image-detectors/).

## When not to rely on the tool

School, work, legal, insurance, or news decisions need a real process — not a free label on a WhatsApp image. Read [why AI image checkers are not legal proof](/blog/why-ai-image-checkers-are-not-legal-proof/), the [FAQ](/faq/), and [Terms](/terms/).

Yes, you can run a check on a screenshot or social photo. It is **less reliable** than the original file. Prefer the original when you can; use [Image Identifier](/) for a private, everyday second opinion.
