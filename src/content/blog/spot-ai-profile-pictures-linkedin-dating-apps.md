Synthetic headshots are everywhere. On **LinkedIn**, recruiters and sales bots use AI headshots to look professional without paying for a photographer. On **dating apps** like Tinder and Bumble, scammers use generator tools to create convincing profiles that look like real people.

When a profile photo looks a little "too perfect," how do you tell if it is an actual human or an **AI-generated headshot**?

You can test any avatar with our [free AI image checker](/). Before you make a decision, here are the most reliable visual clues and what a check can tell you.

## 5 visual signs of an AI-generated profile picture

Modern generators (like Midjourney, FLUX, and StyleGAN models) are good at faces. They still make subtle mistakes that real camera lenses do not produce:

### 1. Eye reflections and pupil shape
In real photography, the light reflection (catchlight) in both pupils matches because both eyes look at the same light source. AI faces often have:
- Asymmetrical reflections (a circle in one pupil, a square or blob in the other)
- Slightly non-circular or warped pupils
- Eyes pointing in microscopically conflicting directions

### 2. Jewelry, glasses, and symmetry traps
Human accessories follow physical rules. AI generators treat them as patterns:
- **Earrings:** One ear has a dangling pearl earring; the other ear has a stud or a melted blob of metal.
- **Eyeglasses:** Frames that dissolve into the cheek, nose pads that do not connect to the bridge, or mismatched temple arms behind the ears.
- **Teeth:** An extra tooth, blurred incisors, or an unnaturally uniform "wall of white" without individual tooth definition.

### 3. Background blur (surreal bokeh)
Real portrait lenses produce smooth, optical background blur (bokeh). AI generators often create:
- Random geometric shapes, floating objects, or distorted text in the background that cannot be identified
- Inconsistent depth of field (e.g., hair strands in focus, but a collar at the same depth completely smeared)

### 4. Collar lines and clothing seams
AI headshots often paste a synthetic head onto a business suit template:
- Lapels that do not meet evenly at the neck
- Tie patterns that dissolve or change direction halfway down
- Seams and fabric weaves that vanish into smooth plastic patches

### 5. Overly polished "wax" skin texture
Look closely at the forehead and cheeks:
- Completely uniform skin tone with zero natural pores, blemishes, or fine lines
- A glowing, airbrushed sheen that looks more like a 3D video game render than a camera sensor
