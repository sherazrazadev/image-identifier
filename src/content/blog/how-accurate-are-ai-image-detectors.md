If you searched **how accurate are AI image detectors**, the honest answer is: **helpful, not perfect**. Free checkers — including [Image Identifier](/) — can flag many AI-looking images and many real photos correctly. They can also be wrong both ways.

That is why our result labels say **Likely** AI-generated or **Likely** real, and why we never call a check legal proof.

## What “accurate” means for a free checker

Accuracy is not one number you can trust forever. It depends on:

- How the image was made (newer tools change often)
- How the file was shared (crop, compress, screenshot, repost)
- How clear the picture is (tiny or blurry files are harder)
- What you need the answer for (curiosity vs a high-stakes decision)

A tool that feels “right” on social media samples can still miss a carefully edited photo, or flag a real camera shot that looks unusual.

## Ways detectors get it wrong

| Mistake | What it looks like | What you should do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| False positive | Real photo labeled AI-generated | Do not accuse anyone on a label alone |
| False negative | AI image labeled real | Stay skeptical of viral claims |
| Unclear | No confident lean either way | Try another source or ask for the original file |

Heavy compression, memes with big text overlays, and multi-generation screenshots are common problem cases for any public checker.

## Accuracy vs certainty

People want a yes/no stamp. Search engines and everyday users often ask for “100% accurate AI detector.” That product does not exist in a free web tool form you can trust for every file forever.

What you *can* get:

- A fast second opinion
- A simple AI vs real lean
- Clear limits in plain language

What you should not expect:

- Court-ready proof
- A guarantee against brand-new generators
- Perfect answers on tiny or heavily re-saved images

## Practical checklist before you trust a result

- Does the claim matter only casually, or could someone get hurt by a wrong call?
- Do you have the original file, or only a repost?
- Did you read the [FAQ](/faq/) disclaimer?
- Would you still verify the story with a second source?

If the answer to the last two is no, treat the detector output as optional context only.
